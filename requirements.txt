====== Requirements and scope ======

===== Product goal =====

PatternSurface generates visually coherent patterns on a parametrized surface whose parameter domain is subdivided into time-dependent regions. Each region may use a different pattern-generation process, while shared boundaries can impose relationships between adjacent processes.

===== Required mathematical behavior =====

  - The ambient space is R^3.
  - A surface patch is represented by r:D->R^3, with D a subset of R^2.
  - A plane is represented as a valid special case of the surface abstraction.
  - The original parameter coordinates (u,v) are the canonical coordinates for subdivision and pattern generation.
  - Pattern generators may define derived coordinates (h,k)=psi(u,v,t).
  - A partition may contain arbitrary supported regions, shared boundaries and adjacency relationships.
  - Region geometry may move continuously.
  - Regions may be created, destroyed, split, and merged through explicit lifecycle events.
  - Pattern methods may have continuous-time, discrete-time, static, or stochastic behavior.
  - Boundary conditions are configurable by interface and may be exact or soft.
  - Parameter-space operators and intrinsic surface operators must be distinguishable.
  - Geometry, field state, topology, constraints, numerical discretization and rendering must remain separate concepts.

===== First-release functional requirements =====

  - Surface definitions: plane and cylinder; interface permits future patches.
  - Partition: create and edit regions; query adjacency; validate geometry; perform transactional split/merge/delete operations.
  - Patterns: vector-field curves, one reaction-diffusion PDE generator, and one cellular automaton.
  - Constraints: support at least one exact geometric condition and one soft compatibility objective; detect unsupported or contradictory policies.
  - Dynamics: controlled boundary motion and scripted topology events with state-transfer policies.
  - Coordinate maps: identity and configurable mapping to (h,k), including validity/capability checks.
  - UI: edit parameter-space geometry, inspect regions and parameters, preview in 3D, control simulation, inspect errors.
  - CLI: validate, simulate and export without GUI startup.
  - Persistence: versioned project format and reproducible random seeds.
  - Testing: unit, geometry invariant, numerical and integration coverage.

===== Non-functional requirements =====

  - C++20 on Linux.
  - CMake-based reproducible builds.
  - Headless core library; no Qt dependency from core public headers.
  - Stable IDs and explicit ownership; no ambiguous pointer lifetime across topology changes.
  - Useful diagnostics for invalid input, solver failure, infeasible constraints and unsupported generator combinations.
  - Testable abstractions and deterministic behavior where practical.
  - Third-party licenses reviewed before distribution.
  - Avoid premature GPU dependence; CPU execution is the initial baseline.

===== Explicitly deferred =====

  - Full arbitrary-surface atlas simulation.
  - Universal solver that can automatically solve any PDE.
  - Arbitrary scripting language and external binary plugin ABI.
  - Guaranteed exact geometry for every conceivable implicit or fractal boundary.
  - GPU compute as a requirement.
  - Manufacturing/fabrication certification.

Deferred items should remain possible through abstractions but are not acceptance criteria for the first release.

===== Design constraints =====

The partition representation must preserve shared-boundary identity. Generators cannot directly mutate the authoritative topology; they request changes through the partition/event API. Rendering must never define the authoritative geometry. A numerical grid is a discretization of the mathematical domain, not the domain itself.
