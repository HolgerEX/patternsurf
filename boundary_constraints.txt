====== Boundary constraints and coupling ======

===== Shared interface model =====

For adjacent regions i and j, let Gamma_ij be their shared interface. The partition owns its identity and geometry. Each generator provides whatever boundary trace/state is meaningful for its output type, using a declared convention for parameter direction and orientation.

Do not apply one universal notion of continuity to all generator types. Value continuity for a scalar field, curve tangent matching, cellular-automaton neighbor exchange, and minimum clearance are different mathematical conditions.

===== Policy categories =====

^ Category ^ Example ^ Typical use ^
| Value matching | q_i = q_j on Gamma_ij | continuous scalar/vector fields or color fields |
| Flux conservation | J_i dot n_i + J_j dot n_j = 0 | transported/diffusive quantities with defined flux |
| Derivative matching | specified normal/tangential derivative relation | smooth field transitions |
| Curve continuity | endpoints and optionally tangent directions agree | vector-field curves and geometric strokes |
| Phase/alignment | motifs share phase, orientation or anchor points | periodic grids and tilings |
| Exclusion/clearance | cross-boundary distance >= d_min | particles, dots and fabrication patterns |
| Discrete neighbor exchange | sites across a boundary are neighbors under a mapping | cellular automata |
| Soft compatibility | minimize a boundary mismatch functional | aesthetic harmony where exact equality is not required |

The mathematical convention of a policy must state whether it uses parameter-space geometry or physical surface geometry. Normal orientation and the correspondence between both sides must be explicit.

===== Hard and soft constraints =====

A hard constraint is an invariant or equation that must hold within a configured numerical tolerance. A soft constraint contributes an objective or penalty and can remain unsatisfied. Never silently convert a hard condition to a soft one to make a solver appear successful.

A general residual is C_ij(state_i, state_j, interface, parameters). A soft energy may be E=sum over interfaces of integral(weight * norm(C_ij)^2) ds. The measure ds and the residual norm must be defined for each policy; parameter-space arclength and physical surface arclength are not interchangeable.

===== Solver responsibilities =====

The boundary subsystem:
  - validates whether a policy is supported by both adjacent generators;
  - supplies a consistent interface correspondence;
  - evaluates hard-condition residuals and soft objectives;
  - reports convergence, infeasibility and conflicts;
  - respects policy priority/locking and configured tolerances;
  - never mutates topology as an undocumented side effect.

The subsystem need not use one universal solver. Exact construction, constrained optimization, PDE interface assembly and discrete neighbor mapping can be separate policy implementations.

===== Initial acceptance scope =====

Implement at least:
  * a geometric curve interface condition suitable for matched endpoints and optional tangent directions;
  * one soft compatibility objective with inspectable residual/score;
  * unsupported-combination checks;
  * explicit conflict/failure reporting;
  * tests where constraints succeed and tests where they are contradictory.

Add field-value and flux conditions with the PDE milestone, and cross-region cellular-automaton neighbor exchange with the cellular-automaton milestone.

===== Diagnostics =====

Each condition result should identify the interface, policy ID, hard/soft classification, measured residual/score, tolerance/target, convergence state, and diagnostic message. Include enough information for the UI to display which boundary failed and why.
